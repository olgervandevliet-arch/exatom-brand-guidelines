UI elements

The building blocks the website is assembled from. Radii, weights and colors here are the defaults — deviate only with reason.

Buttons

One primary action per view, repeated down the page rather than competing with alternatives beside it. Blue is the only filled action colour; Dark is reserved for a second, quieter primary on light sections.

999px radius13 / 24px paddingsmall: 9 / 18px at 13pxInter SemiBold 15px−2% tracking1px borderno shadowhover: label roll 420ms, colour holdstertiary: underline

Primary, Secondary, Dark, Tertiary, Disabled, each at both sizes. Tertiary is text alone — no fill, no border, no arrow. It carries the third-most-important action on a view, and never the only one. On hover the label drops out of the bottom while a second copy arrives from above, clipped by the pill — one 420ms move. Nothing else changes: fill, border and text colour hold, so the roll is the whole of the feedback. Tertiary is the exception — with no fill to clip against it underlines instead. Disabled does not respond at all, and neither does anything when the visitor asks for reduced motion.

On dark

On Dark #18122D the roles stay the same but the colours invert. Blue is not usable as a fill here: at 2.6:1 against this ground it fails contrast, so the primary action becomes white and Tertiary switches to Blue Light.

PrimaryWhite fill, Dark text. The only filled action on the surface.
SecondaryTransparent, 1px white at 28%, white text. The border lifts to 50% on hover.
TertiaryBlue Light #E8EEFF text, no fill. Blue #2A5AE9 is never used for text on Dark.

What we hold ourselves to

Drawn from the friction points we find in client forms week after week. If our own form breaks one of these, it is a bug.

DO

  • Label above the field, always visible while typing.
  • Validate when the field is left, not on every keystroke.
  • Say what to do: “Add the domain ending”, not “Invalid input”.
  • Derive what you can — address from postcode, company from email domain.
  • One column. Two columns only for values that belong together, like postcode and number.
  • Keep the submit button reachable without scrolling past the last field.

SKIP

  • Placeholder text standing in for the label — it disappears exactly when it is needed.
  • Asterisks on required fields when nearly everything is required. Mark the optional ones.
  • Clearing what someone typed because the format was wrong.
  • Asking twice for something you already have.
  • A reset button next to submit.
  • Error summaries at the top with no link down to the field.
